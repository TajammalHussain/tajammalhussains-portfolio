problem: >
  Supplier payments, subcontractor payment certificates and CIS payment
  sheets were produced through manual steps and manual approval chains. Each
  document had to be assembled by hand, passed between people for sign-off,
  and reconciled before payment, and the process depended on a dedicated
  administrative role to keep it moving. There was no single structured
  record of what had been requested, approved and paid.
constraints: >
  Subcontractor payments carry CIS, HMRC and BACS requirements, so the
  calculations and outputs had to be correct and traceable rather than
  approximately right. Approvals needed multiple authorisation levels and a
  signature that could be captured digitally instead of on paper. The
  solution also had to be owned end to end: designed around the business's
  own process, not bent to fit a generic tool's data model, and structured so
  new payment types could be added later without a redesign.
approach: >
  I engineered the suite as a standalone, custom-built system rather than a
  configuration of an existing product. The starting point was the schema: a
  tailored data model designed for operational efficiency, with separate,
  well-defined entities for requests, approvals, certificates and payment
  outputs. On top of it I built a bespoke workflow engine that moves each
  record through defined stages (submission, multi-level authorisation,
  e-signature, payment output) and a logic layer that applies the CIS, HMRC
  and BACS rules inside the system, so the correct figures and file outputs
  are produced by the process itself instead of being assembled by hand.
architecture: >
  A modular process-automation framework with three layers that are
  deliberately independent of one another: a custom data model, a logic layer
  (calculation rules and validation), and a workflow and approval pipeline
  that routes records and captures e-signatures. Supplier payments,
  subcontractor payment certificates and CIS payment sheets are three
  workflows built on the same framework and share one approval and
  documentation pattern. Because the schema and logic are not tied to any
  external platform, the architecture is platform-agnostic and can be
  extended with new workflows without reworking the foundation.
outcome: >
  The suite eliminated a full administrative role and replaced manual
  approval chains with structured digital workflows, including e-signature
  and multi-level authorisation. Every payment request, certificate and
  payment sheet now lives in a structured record with a clear approval
  history, in place of documents passed between people.
retrospective: >
  The natural next step is to extend the same framework to further payment
  and approval workflows as new modules, reusing the existing schema, logic
  layer and approval pipeline rather than starting each one from scratch.

## Architecture

The suite is easiest to understand as two flows: a payment calculation flow
that produces a payable amount, and an approval workflow that routes the
resulting certificate.

### Subcontractor payment calculation flow

<MermaidDiagram
  label="Subcontractor payment calculation flow feeding an approval workflow"
  chart={`flowchart TD
  DB[("Subcontractor\ndatabase")] --> SEL["Select subcontractor"]
  SEL --> PREV["Gross previously\npaid amount"]
  PREV --> DUE{"Gross payment due"}

  DUE -->|"greater than zero"| TOT["Total gross amount"]
  TOT --> RETSEL["Retention option:\nrate A / rate B / none / manual entry"]
  RETSEL --> PBT["Payment before tax"]
  RETSEL -.-> ACC(("Accumulated retention\ncalculated and stored"))
  ACC -.-> DB

  DUE -->|"equal to zero"| REL(("Accumulated retention\nprocessed for release"))
  DB -.-> REL
  REL --> RELSEL["Release option:\nnone / partial tier 1 / partial tier 2"]
  RELSEL --> PTR["Total retention\namount payable"]

  PBT --> MAT["Material cost"]
  PTR --> MAT

  MAT --> V1["1. Standard-rate VAT"]
  MAT --> V2["2. Zero-rated"]
  MAT --> V3["3. Reverse charge"]

  V1 --> R1["VAT payable +\ntaxable payment"]
  V2 --> R2["VAT payable +\ntaxable payment"]
  V3 --> R3["VAT payable to tax authority +\ntaxable payment to tax authority"]

  R1 --> C1{"CIS tax deduction"}
  R2 --> C2{"CIS tax deduction"}
  R3 --> C3{"CIS tax deduction"}

  C1 -->|"standard rate"| PAY["Payable amount"]
  C1 -->|"zero"| PAY
  C2 -->|"standard rate"| PAY
  C2 -->|"zero"| PAY
  C3 -->|"standard rate"| PAY
  C3 -->|"zero"| PAY

  PAY --> CERT["Payment certificate\nsubmitted by user"]
  CERT --> AP1["Approver 1"]
  CERT --> AP2["Approver 2"]
  AP1 --> GATE{"Both approved?"}
  AP2 --> GATE
  GATE -->|"yes"| FIN["Final approver\nreceives approval email"]`}
/>

The calculation branches on whether a payment is due. When it is, the system
applies the selected retention option, stores the accumulated retention against
the subcontractor record, then applies the VAT treatment (standard, zero-rated
or reverse charge) and the CIS deduction to arrive at the payable amount. When
nothing is due, the same tax branches run against the accumulated retention
being released. The payable amount becomes a payment certificate, which only
reaches the final approver once both approvers have approved.

### Approval workflow with configurable bypass

<MermaidDiagram
  label="Approval workflow with configurable bypass options"
  chart={`flowchart TD
  S{"Bypass any\napproval stage?"}

  S -->|"1"| O1["No bypass\nemail sent to Approver 1, then Approver 2"]
  S -->|"2"| O2["Bypass Approver 1\nemail sent to Approver 2"]
  S -->|"3"| O3["Bypass Approver 2\nemail sent to Approver 1"]
  S -->|"4"| O4["Bypass both approvers\nemail sent to final approver"]

  O1 --> A1["Approver 1 approval"]
  A1 --> A2["Approver 2 approval"]
  O2 --> A2b["Approver 2 approval"]
  O3 --> A1b["Approver 1 approval"]

  A2 -->|"email sent to final approver"| FIN["Final approval\nemail received by final approver"]
  A2b -->|"email sent to final approver"| FIN
  A1b -->|"email sent to final approver"| FIN
  O4 -->|"email sent to final approver"| FIN`}
/>

Each certificate is submitted with a bypass option that decides which approval
stages apply. Whichever route is chosen, the record always ends at the final
approver, and each stage sends its own email notification.

The design decision that mattered most was building the schema and logic layer
as the foundation and treating each payment workflow as a module on top of it.
Supplier payments, subcontractor certificates and CIS payment sheets differ in
their rules but share the same approval and documentation pattern, so a new
workflow means adding a module, not redesigning the system.
